Alley Cat

A reimagining of Bill Williams' 1984 game. Two screens rendered here — the title and the courtyard (phase 0) — keeping the shape of the CGA original but drawing everything procedurally at 3× the resolution, with lighting, animation, and colour the 1984 hardware could not give it.

How to play

Attract mode. The alley at night. A cat patrols the fence, sits, licks its paw, and every seven seconds a different cat pops out of a trash can to fling a fish bone onto the pavement. Nothing to do here — just watch. Click a tab above to visit a phase.

The courtyard (art demo). The sitting room that opens every game in the original. Fireplace flames flicker, a cat curls up on the rug, the mantle clock ticks. Not playable yet.

The pond (art demo). Underwater view — colourful ornamental fish swim across the frame, bubbles rise, sunbeams angle through the water. Not playable yet.

The library (art demo). Books line the shelves, a small fire crackles on the left wall. Not playable yet.

🐭 Objective: The cat starts on the floor. Climb up the cheese wall by leaping from hole to hole — or use the furniture on the right as extra footholds. When a mouse pops out of the hole the cat is CLINGING to, it's caught automatically.

⌨️ Keyboard:

  • On the floor: hold ←→ (or AD) to walk anywhere in the room. Press ↑ to leap up to the nearest bottom-row cheese hole.
  • On the wall: ←→ hops sideways one hole; ↑ climbs one level up; ↓ drops to the nearest hole below (or, from the bottom row, drops back to the floor).
  • On a perch (chair or table): ↑ leaps higher (chair seat → chair back → cheese hole); ↓ drops to the floor.

🖱️ Mouse: Click any hole, chair seat, chair back or the side table top and the cat leaps toward it.

Movement rules:

  • Downward leaps are free — the cat can drop from the top row all the way to the bottom in one hop.
  • Upward leaps between cheese holes are limited to one level at a time.
  • Every leap has a maximum reach. Trying to leap too far — sideways, too many levels up, or to a target outside range — the cat clearly tries to make the jump, comes up short, and falls to the floor stunned.
  • The chair back reaches higher than any single hole climb — from there you can leap to holes several levels up. Use it to reach the top row quickly.
  • 🏺 The vase on the side table breaks the first time the cat lands on the table — shards and flowers scatter on the floor. This is just a bit of chaos; it does not affect your score.
  • The hole the cat is CLINGING to will never spawn a mouse.

Scoring: +1 per catch. Every three consecutive catches raises your combo multiplier (+2 each after 3-in-a-row, +3 after 6, and so on). A missed mouse or a fall breaks the combo. Your best score is saved between visits.

The mouse spawn rate speeds up as your score climbs.

What the original looked like

CGA mode 4: 320×200, four colours — magenta, cyan, white, black. The title screen showed a wooden fence with five trash cans, a tiny cat silhouette on the fence, and the game name in cursive script. Phase 0 was a sparse room: two chairs, a table, a small round table, a floor lamp, and a fireplace with a black opening.

Both frames are captured in reference/screens/; the reconstruction rebuilds byte-identically and every reference frame here came from the rebuild, not the shipped binary.

What this port changes

No file matches the original pixel for pixel. The arrangement is preserved — same furniture in the same places, same fence composition — but the palette is unshackled, the surfaces are shaded, the fireplace burns, the lampshade actually casts light, and the trash cans look like metal instead of stacked hoops.

Everything on the canvas is drawn from JavaScript. No image files, no external fonts loaded at runtime beyond the browser's own cursive fallback. Open index.html directly — no build step, no server.
