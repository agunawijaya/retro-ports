Alley Cat

A reimagining of Bill Williams' 1984 game. Eight scenes rendered here — the title and all seven phases — keeping the shape of the CGA original but drawing everything procedurally at 3× the resolution, with lighting, animation, and colour the 1984 hardware could not give it. Phase 4 (the cheese wall) and Phase 5 (the birdcage) are playable; the other five are art demos.

How to play

Attract mode. The alley at night. A cat patrols the fence, sits, licks its paw, and every seven seconds a different cat pops out of a trash can to fling a fish bone onto the pavement. Nothing to do here — just watch. Click a tab above to visit a phase.

The courtyard (art demo). The sitting room that opens every game in the original. Fireplace flames flicker, a cat curls up on the rug, the mantle clock ticks. Not playable yet.

The pond (art demo). Underwater view — colourful ornamental fish swim across the frame, bubbles rise, sunbeams angle through the water. Not playable yet.

The library (art demo). Books line the shelves, a small fire crackles on the left wall. Not playable yet.

🐭 Objective: Climb the cheese wall by jumping from hole to hole and using the chair and side table as extra footholds. When a mouse pops out of the hole the cat is CLINGING to, it's caught automatically.

⌨️ Controls (platformer + grab):

  • Hold ←→ (or AD) to walk on the floor, along the chair seat, along the chair back, or along the side table top.
  • Press ↑ to jump straight up. Hold ← or → before pressing ↑ for a diagonal jump (about 34° from vertical, much more horizontal reach). If you press ←/→ after jumping, air control lets you steer mid-flight — weaker than a proper diagonal but useful for correction.
  • Press ↓ to drop through the furniture platform you're standing on (falls to whatever's below, or the floor). Hold ←/→ for a diagonal drop — same horizontal boost as the diagonal jump, but going down.
  • Walking off an edge falls — gravity takes over the moment your paws leave the platform.

On a cheese hole (hanging):

  • The cat automatically grabs the nearest hole its jump arc passes close to (while descending or at apex). To reach an upper hole, aim your jump underneath it.
  • ↑ jumps up off the hole (with an optional diagonal boost from ←/→).
  • ← or → alone: lateral hop — a small sideways-and-up hop that may grab an adjacent hole.
  • ↓ releases the hole and drops (straight or diagonally if ←/→ is held).

Fall damage: falling more than ~100 px onto the floor stuns the cat for 0.7 s (dizzy pose, no input). Combo breaks.

🖱️ Mouse: click anywhere and the cat walks toward that x on its current surface. Vertical navigation uses the keyboard.

Vase: 🏺 the first time the cat lands on the side table, the vase tumbles off the edge, hits the floor with a puff of dust, and shatters — flowers, stems and shards scatter around the table. Just chaos; doesn't affect your score.

Scoring: +1 per catch. Every three consecutive catches raises your combo multiplier (+2 each after 3-in-a-row, +3 after 6, and so on). A missed mouse or a stunned fall breaks the combo. Your best is saved between visits.

Mouse spawn rate speeds up as your score climbs.

🐦 Objective: Jump onto the centre table — that knocks the cage off, the door swings open, and the bird escapes. Chase it around the room; catch it mid-flight or by landing on whichever piece of furniture it just settled on. Fewer hops is better.

⌨️ Controls (platformer):

  • Hold ←→ (or AD) to walk — on the floor, along the top of the table, along a chair seat, along a chair back. Every furniture surface is a platform.
  • Press ↑ to jump straight up. If there's a platform above where you land, you catch it (one-way collision — you can rise through platforms from below and land on them from above).
  • Hold ← or → before pressing ↑ for a diagonal jump (~34° from vertical). If you press ←/→ after jumping, air control lets you steer mid-flight.
  • Press ↓ to drop through the platform you're standing on (falls to whatever's below, or the floor). Hold ←/→ for a diagonal drop.
  • Walking off an edge falls (Mario style) — gravity takes over the moment your paws leave the platform.

Jump reach. One jump clears ~112 px of vertical: floor → chair seat, chair seat → chair back. Floor → chair back is too far in one hop — step through the chair seat first, or take a diagonal from the table.

🖱️ Mouse: click anywhere and the cat walks toward that x on its current surface. Falling off the edge to reach it is normal. Use the keyboard for vertical jumps.

The bird: after escaping, it flies between landing spots — some the cat can reach (chairs, table), some it can't (fireplace mantle, portrait tops, floor-lamp shade). When the bird chooses an unreachable spot, the only way to catch it is to intercept mid-flight — jump up and cross its path.

Scoring: Hops counts each ↑ and ↓ press. Walking is free. Lower is better; your best is saved between visits.

The kittens (art demo). Kittens and milk bowls on the floor. Not playable yet.

The finale (autopilot demo). Cupids around the border shoot arrows down at a field of hearts — pink hearts are safe to stand on, black ones are broken and drop the cat through. A cat wanders the platform, walking, leaping between platforms, and falling through broken hearts. Not player-controlled yet.

What the original looked like

CGA mode 4: 320×200, four colours — magenta, cyan, white, black. The title screen showed a wooden fence with five trash cans, a tiny cat silhouette on the fence, and the game name in cursive script. Phase 0 was a sparse room: two chairs, a table, a small round table, a floor lamp, and a fireplace with a black opening.

Both frames are captured in reference/screens/; the reconstruction rebuilds byte-identically and every reference frame here came from the rebuild, not the shipped binary.

What this port changes

No file matches the original pixel for pixel. The arrangement is preserved — same furniture in the same places, same fence composition — but the palette is unshackled, the surfaces are shaded, the fireplace burns, the lampshade actually casts light, and the trash cans look like metal instead of stacked hoops.

Everything on the canvas is drawn from JavaScript. No image files, no external fonts loaded at runtime beyond the browser's own cursive fallback. Open index.html directly — no build step, no server.
